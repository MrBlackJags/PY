Chronology of Loss

A Timeline of Extinction Waves

Follow how shifting climates, island colonization, and modern industrialization reshaped our planet's biodiversity over thousands of years.

c. 10,000 – 8,000 BCE

The Quaternary Megafaunal Collapse

At the close of the last glacial maximum, Earth experienced dramatic climatic shifts. Tundra-steppes transformed into forests and wetlands, shrinking the grazing ranges of massive herbivores. As modern human hunter-gatherers spread across Eurasia and the Americas, large mammals with long reproductive cycles could not withstand the joint pressure.

Notable Victims: Woolly Mammoth, Sabre-toothed Cat (Smilodon), Irish Elk, Woolly Rhinoceros, Giant Ground Sloth.
c. 1500s – 1700s

The Age of Exploration & Island Fragility

Global maritime navigation led European explorers and merchant ships to isolated oceanic islands (Mauritius, Madagascar, Commander Islands). Island fauna had evolved over millennia without terrestrial mammalian predators, lacking fear or defensive evasion tactics. Introduced rats, pigs, cats, and goats destroyed breeding grounds, while sailors restocked ship provisions.

Notable Victims: The Dodo (1662), Steller's Sea Cow (1768, eliminated in just 27 years), Elephant Birds of Madagascar.
c. 1800s – Mid 1900s

Industrial Harvest & Mass Deforestation

The expansion of railroads, firearms, telegraph communication, and mechanical logging commercialized the hunting of wild animals on an unprecedented scale. Abundance proved to be an illusion: species that numbered in the hundreds of millions were annihilated in decades as market hunters fed growing cities and fashion trades demand for feathers, furs, and leather.

Notable Victims: Passenger Pigeon (1914), Quagga (1883), Great Auk (1844), Tasmanian Tiger / Thylacine (1936), Carolina Parakeet (1918).
Present Day & Future

The Anthropocene & The Call to Action

Today, extinction rates are estimated to be 100 to 1,000 times higher than background natural rates. Scientists recognize this as the onset of the Earth's Sixth Mass Extinction. However, unlike past crises, humans have the scientific tools, genetic understanding, and global awareness to enact rapid conservation, habitat restoration, and legal protections.

Modern Success Stories: Rescues of the American Bison, California Condor, and Przewalski's Horse prove that concerted human efforts can reverse the tide.
